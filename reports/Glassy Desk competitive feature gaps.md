# Sell the moment agents get stuck

Glassy Desk is the cheapest serious way to control a Mac from an iPhone or iPad, but it is not yet the most capable, and its best-known promise is only half built. Its lifetime price of $29.99 is about 15% of Screens 5's $199.99, and its yearly price is a quarter of Astropad Workbench's, yet **Jump Desktop 10 now offers audio, a device-matched virtual display and no-VPN reach for a $14.99 one-time iOS price**. Four parity gaps matter most: no host audio, no virtual display, no reach without Tailscale, and no Mac-to-phone clipboard. A fifth problem is self-inflicted: the paywall says "Watch builds, renders, and AI agents finish", but the app has no notifications or Live Activities, so it cannot tell anyone a job has finished. The exploitable ground is real: Apple still has no documented iPhone or iPad client for its own Screen Sharing, Screens cannot carry audio, agent apps supervise the conversation and not the machine, FileVault reboots remain unsolved in any client found, and silent connection failures and lost keystrokes draw steady complaints. The recommended strategy is to close the display and audio gaps, then own the moment the chat runs out: a private alert that opens the exact Mac screen needing a human. Hold the hosted relay behind a measured decision, because it conflicts with the "no cloud relay" and no-account claims and with lifetime pricing. Evidence is thin in specific ways, including no Reddit access and almost no independent performance testing, so the rankings below are judgment informed by evidence, not measured demand.

## Jump is the feature threat, Workbench the message threat, and Glass the free floor

Glassy Desk competes with three different kinds of rival. Jump competes on capability, Workbench on the same agent-watching story, and Glass, a free MIT-licensed project, on the technical design. Screens remains the closest feature twin and the easiest one to beat on price. The table below shows where each stands; "not found" means the notes did not surface the feature, not that it is proven absent. Glassy Desk's column comes from the source verification supplied for this report on 2026-11-03, plus a limited source check made while writing it.

Jump's strength is documented on its [App Store page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/jump-desktop-rdp-vnc-fluid/id364876095) and [pricing page](https://www.jumpdesktop.com/pricing-plans.html): virtual display, up to 120 fps on ProMotion and audio on iOS. Its most advanced items sit behind Teams Enterprise at $14.99 per user per month, including 60 Mbps, 10-bit color, up to four virtual displays and Pencil pressure. Jump is also moving fast, with build 10.17.1 released about 3 November 2026, which makes "abandoned app" reviews from earlier in the year [probably stale](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=364876095&country=us). Its weak spots are clear in reviews from the same period: [six separate reports of background battery drain](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id364876095), though a 10-minute background default in the [Jump 10 betas](https://changelog.jumpdesktop.com/jump-desktop-10-beta-QXilW) looks like a response, plus complaints about removed pinch-to-zoom and keys that fail in Terminal. No evidence of Duo support surfaced.

Workbench is the direct message threat. [Its product page](https://astropad.com/product/workbench/) sells a matched virtual display, mini-map, dictation, curtain, "intelligent sleep" and a relay, all aimed at people running agents on a Mac. Its price appears to have risen from [$10 a month or $50 a year at launch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/08/astropads-workbench-reimagines-remote-desktop-for-ai-agents-not-it-support/) to [$14.99 or $79.99](https://astropad.com/product/workbench/), and it holds a [4.83 rating from 217 reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6758788573&country=us). Its users also [ask for one-time pricing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758788573) and complain about sign-outs, a reconnect after 15 seconds away, and [Esc closing the session](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758788573). Glassy Desk had one App Store rating as of the [7 October internal review](../docs/md/app-store-competitive-review-2026-10-07.md), which means social proof is a gap of its own, and `requestReview` is still not built.

Screens is exposed on price and transport. Edovia's [FAQ and App Store purchases](https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/s5-pricing) show the prices in the table; older FAQ copies with lower prices appear stale, because [reviewers now cite $199](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912). Screens attributes its lack of audio to [the RFB protocol](https://edovia.com/en/screens/). However, Screens is ahead on Apple integration, with two-way rich clipboard, [Live Activities for transfers](https://blog.edovia.com/screens-57-now-available), dictation and, since June 2026, [remote access built into Screens for Mac](https://blog.edovia.com/) with no separate install. Reviewers also credit it with [Duo support within 24 hours](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912), so Duo is a lead over Jump, RustDesk, AnyDesk and TeamViewer but not over Screens or Workbench.

Glass deserves more attention than its [164 stars](https://github.com/Ntarasiuk/glass) suggest. It captures with ScreenCaptureKit, encodes with VideoToolbox, sends frames as datagrams so lost packets do not delay newer ones, and [claims about 20 ms](https://github.com/Ntarasiuk/glass), plus [audio, microphone and scoped one-time links](https://glassdesk.dev/). These are vendor claims with no independent test, and a browser viewer gives up native keyboard capture, background behavior and App Store discovery. Its domain, glassdesk.dev, is also close enough to Glassy Desk to justify a search-confusion and trademark check. Elsewhere, [Parsec lists no official iOS client](https://parsec.app/downloads), [Sunshine on macOS is labeled experimental with no gamepads](https://docs.lizardbyte.dev/projects/sunshine/latest/md_docs_2getting__started.html), and Splashtop's [Pencil pressure and tilt require a Windows streamer](https://support-splashtopbusiness.splashtop.com/hc/en-us/articles/360055551332-Remote-Stylus-Apple-Pencil-Windows-iOS-Android). Those products are not close threats on the Mac-host, iOS-client path.

Apple sets the ceiling. High Performance Screen Sharing offers [stereo audio, HDR, 4:4:4 and virtual displays, but only Mac to Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/remote-desktop/apdf8e09f5a9/mac). The absence of an iOS client is inferred from documentation and coverage, not stated by Apple, but it is a standing grievance: one Hacker News commenter calls [macOS remote access "absolute sadness"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48858352). If Apple ships an iPad client, this entire category narrows, which is a reason to build value beyond the stream itself.

## Five gaps cost the most, and one is a broken promise

**The missing completion signal is the most urgent gap because Glassy Desk created it.** The paywall sells watching jobs finish, yet the only way to know is to open the app and look. Meanwhile, alerts have become inexpensive and expected: [GitHub Mobile shows agent states in a Live Activity](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-08-github-mobile-live-notifications-for-copilot-cli-sessions/), and [Agentfy charges $2.99 a month, $19.99 a year or $39.99 lifetime](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6757966311) for little beyond alerts. That lifetime price exceeds Glassy Desk's lifetime price for the entire remote desktop. The hooks are public: [Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks) and [Codex](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/hooks) both expose `PermissionRequest` and `Stop`, and a plain "run this when done" command would cover renders and builds that no agent hook sees.

**The virtual display is table stakes for the audience the product courts.** A headless Apple silicon Mac [defaults to non-Retina 1920×1080, per Astropad](https://astropad.com/blog/dummy-plug-headless-mac-mini/), which is a vendor source but matches user reports of [fuzzy and misfit displays](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912). Reviewers [praise Jump's result](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id364876095) and [Workbench's](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758788573). Matching alone no longer differentiates, so Glassy Desk should aim for Apollo's model of [a remembered display per client](https://github.com/ClassicOldSong/Apollo), which is Windows-only there. Host distribution helps: the host ships as a [notarized Developer ID app](../docs/md/launch-readiness-2026-09-07.md), so the private `CGVirtualDisplay` API is a technical and maintenance risk, not an App Review block. That risk is real, since one small fork [shipped a macOS virtual display backend and then removed it](https://github.com/SamuelRioTz/remotedisplay).

**Audio is a low-volume complaint with high strategic value.** Audio drew only 10 of 485 low-rated reviews in the keyword sample, but it is the cleanest single claim against Screens, and Final Cut users [still look for a working path](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255961650). The source check found audio capture disabled and no wire message for it, so the work spans host, protocol and client. One small detail earns trust: a MacRumors user reported that [closing TeamViewer flipped a headless music server's output](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/mac-mini-changes-sound-output.2491260/), so Glassy Desk should never disturb the chosen output device.

**Reach without a VPN is the largest gap for ordinary users, but the most expensive to close.** Screens, Jump and Workbench all offer it, and the next section treats it as a separate decision. A hidden weakness travels with it: the [connectivity plan](../docs/md/remote-host-connectivity-plan.md) says video and input share one TCP connection, and a source check confirmed the host listener is TCP. Over a congested WAN, delayed video can therefore delay keystrokes. Glass's datagram design and [RustDesk's new WebRTC paths and QoS work](https://rustdesk.com/blog/) avoid exactly this, so "fast on my LAN" may not hold over Tailscale on cellular. No test has compared them.

**Mac-to-phone clipboard is pure parity.** Screens, Jump and [Windows App](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-app/compare-platforms-features) all have it. Smaller gaps follow the same pattern: single-window capture has [visible appetite in the 275-point Hacker News response to Sill](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22Any%20window%20from%20your%20Mac%22&tags=story), dictation is [praised by MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/astropad-workbench-rethinks-remote-mac-control-for-ai-agents/), and game controllers exist in Jump but matter little to the Mac-work audience.

## Rivals leave six openings that fit Glassy Desk's design

**Price works against Screens and Workbench, not Jump.** Pricing resentment is the most consistent Screens theme, with paying a subscription for [purely local use](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912) as the specific grievance and Screens 4 owners [doubting any lifetime promise will stick](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912). Glassy Desk should state its lifetime terms in writing and disclose the free limit before the host install, because [surprise after setup](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912) is a recurring trigger. It should not claim to be cheapest overall, since Jump's iOS one-time price is half its lifetime price.

**The 80-second free tier can become an asset, once alerts exist.** Against Workbench's [20 minutes a day](https://astropad.com/product/workbench/) and Screens' [7-day trial](https://edovia.com/en/screens/), 80 seconds is too short to evaluate stream quality, as the internal review already warned when the limit was 60 seconds. No evidence shows how users react to timed tiers. My judgment is that 80 seconds fits one specific job, which is answering a prompt after an alert. A free user who gets pulled in by an alert, clicks "Allow" and leaves has experienced the product's best moment, and the [existing cooldown and paywall events](../docs/md/analytics.md) can measure whether that converts. A longer one-time first session would address the evaluation problem separately.

**Reboot recovery is the sharpest headless wedge.** Tahoe made [SSH unlock of FileVault possible](https://www.jeffgeerling.com/blog/2025/you-can-finally-manage-macs-filevault-remotely-tahoe/) with Remote Login enabled beforehand, but users call the route [a multi-step hassle, and some turn FileVault off](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/tahoe-and-filevault/42942). [Password Utility sells authenticated restart at $10 per device per year](https://9to5mac.com/2026/01/17/password-utility-solves-the-filevault-reboot-headache-for-remote-macs/), which shows people pay for this. macOS 26.5 adds [automatic startup when power returns](https://jeffgeerling.com/blog/2026/power-on-your-mac-remotely) on recent desktops, with FileVault still requiring unlock. The notes found no client that integrates the flow, though that is absence of evidence. Because the [companion runs only after login](../docs/md/app-store-competitive-review-2026-10-07.md), this flow depends on the unverified login-window fallback to Standard VNC, so test that first.

**Telling people why a connection failed is cheap and under-served.** "Cannot connect" drew 55 low-rated hits, and the examples are all alike: [no diagnostics](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912), [a silent bounce to the server list](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id364876095) and [a Mac showing online with no connection or error](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758788573). The plan's first phase already lists reachability and identity diagnostics, so this work is both low-risk and planned.

**Keyboard and pointer fidelity is an unglamorous lead to claim.** Keyboard and pointer issues produced 31 and 29 low-rated hits, across products: [Ctrl and Esc not reaching the host](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id352019548), [Cmd+Q quitting the client](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id364876095), and [the on-screen keyboard covering the desktop](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/astropad-workbench-rethinks-remote-mac-control-for-ai-agents/). Owning the protocol lets Glassy Desk guarantee key delivery in a way that can be tested and shown. Glassy Desk's fixed shortcut strip is a start, not a differentiator.

**Trust is an asset that most rivals have spent.** AnyDesk's recent reviews are [dominated by scam warnings, 58 of 150](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1176131273), and its [2024 breach exposed source code and signing certificates](https://www.securityweek.com/anydesk-revokes-passwords-certificates-in-response-to-hack/amp/). TeamViewer's [commercial-use flagging](https://www.teamviewer.com/en-cis/global/support/knowledge-base/teamviewer-classic/licensing/personal-use/commercial-use-suspected) keeps driving away [personal users](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1581225015). Workbench and Jump users [complain about accounts](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758788573), while [no cloud in the path is praised](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912). Glassy Desk's no-account, no-relay position answers all of this, but it needs a public security page, a key-rotation plan, and a clear statement that it has no incoming-stranger flow. Because Standard VNC uses Apple's service, that page should also address issues like [CVE-2026-65400, flagged by Edovia](https://blog.edovia.com/); only Edovia's excerpt was read, not Apple's advisory.

Treat one tempting claim with care. The 4K60 line is true as a ceiling, but the top preset averages about 12 Mbps. Jump's individual tier allows [20 Mbps](https://www.jumpdesktop.com/pricing-plans.html), and Apple recommends [75 Mbps per 4K display](https://support.apple.com/guide/remote-desktop/apdf8e09f5a9/mac) for its own mode. These figures are not like-for-like, and mostly static desktops compress well, but a motion-heavy head-to-head could embarrass the screenshot. Glass's [motion and still hybrid encoding](https://github.com/Ntarasiuk/glass) points to a better story: sharp text at low bitrate.

## The no-relay pledge can survive if direct paths come first

The internal [connectivity plan](../docs/md/remote-host-connectivity-plan.md) recommends Sign in with Apple, QR enrolment and an opaque regional relay as the default, with direct paths added later. That design is secure, but as a default it would contradict the App Store's "no cloud relay" and no-account claims, and it sits badly with lifetime pricing. The plan's own figures show why: the top preset moves about 5.4 GB per hour. A user relaying 40 hours a month at that preset generates 216 GB; at an illustrative $0.05 per GB, an assumption and not a quoted price, that is about $11 a month against $1.99 of revenue, with lifetime customers contributing nothing further.

The steelman for building it anyway is strong. Tailscale needs two installs, an account and an iOS VPN slot that [conflicts with other VPNs](../docs/md/remote-host-connectivity-plan.md), and small-business operators running agent Macs are not network engineers. Screens' June move also [raised the setup bar](https://blog.edovia.com/). Yet [Tailscale is repeatedly praised by Screens users](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912), and a vocal group wants no vendor in the path, so the gap costs an unknown share of customers. No source gives figures on what share of sessions need a relay.

My recommendation reverses the plan's order, in three stages. First, finish guided Tailscale setup: detect the address, store it with the pairing and explain failures. Second, add direct NAT traversal using a minimal rendezvous service that carries connection candidates and no media, following the pattern in [RustDesk 1.5](https://rustdesk.com/blog/) and [Glass](https://github.com/Ntarasiuk/glass). QUIC or WebRTC here also fixes the TCP problem. The plan is right that CloudKit should not carry low-latency signaling. Third, decide on a relay using measured failures. If guided setup and direct paths together leave more than about a quarter of attempting users unable to connect from outside, a threshold I am proposing and not deriving from data, ship an opt-in opaque relay. It should be a separate paid add-on, excluded from existing lifetime terms, with the listing reworded to "off unless you turn it on; it cannot read your screen." Any rendezvous service changes what the listing can say, so the wording should become precise: your screen and input never pass through our servers.

## Original proposals: eight features rivals are poorly placed to copy

**Everything in this section is proposal and judgment, not sourced fact.** Evidence is cited where it exists, but none of these ideas was validated with users. Effort assumes a small team: S is under two weeks, M is two to six weeks, L is two months or more. "Hard to copy" is relative, since Jump and Workbench are capable teams and most head starts here last months, not years.

**1. Takeover Bell: a private alert that opens the exact screen that needs you.** A Mac-side `glassy notify` command and one-tap Claude Code and Codex hook setup send a push. Tapping it opens the right Mac, zoomed to the relevant window, with a banner stating why you were called. A "Done, hand back" button then signals the waiting script or agent. *Insight:* agent apps stop where the chat stops. [Some Claude prompts appear only where the session runs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control), macOS privacy prompts [need clicking on the Mac](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/channels), and a non-exhaustive search found no remote desktop with a shipped human-agent handoff. *Why rivals lag:* first-party agent vendors have no reason to stream a desktop, and alert apps have no screen; Jump and Glass have MCP, and Workbench is the most likely fast follower. Multi-agent neutrality and a no-account design are the differentiators. *Delivery:* use a very small gateway that holds only device tokens and forwards end-to-end-encrypted payloads to APNs, decrypted by a notification extension. The plan warns that [CloudKit notifications can be delayed or coalesced](../docs/md/remote-host-connectivity-plan.md), and they need the same Apple Account on both ends, though [Vigil's maker describes an iCloud-only route](https://www.producthunt.com/products/vigil-companion) worth a spike. *Effort:* M for alerts and Live Activity, plus M for handback. *Main risk:* macOS may reject synthetic clicks on some security prompts. If injected input cannot answer the prompts the pitch depends on, the core claim fails, so test this before writing marketing copy, on both Glassy Stream and Standard VNC. Agents' own [screenshots and computer use](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/remote-connections) also narrow the need over time.

**2. Screen Sentinel: alerts for jobs that have no hooks.** The user marks a display or region, and the host alerts when it stops changing, changes, or shows chosen text such as "error" using on-device Vision OCR. *Insight:* [checking long renders from a phone](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id692035811) is a real use, and renders have no hook; the host already reads ScreenCaptureKit frame status, including idle frames, so the signal is close to free. *Why rivals lag:* it needs a host helper on the capture path, which rules out a plain VNC client; Jump and Workbench could add it but are focused elsewhere. *Effort:* S to M for stillness, M with OCR. *Main risk:* false alerts erode trust quickly, and capturing with no viewer connected costs power and raises privacy questions.

**3. Native Mac menus on the phone.** The host reads the frontmost app's menu bar through Accessibility, already a required permission, and the client shows real iOS menus and a key strip that follows the active app. *Insight:* phone complaints center on [keys that fail](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758788573), [keyboards covering the screen](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/astropad-workbench-rethinks-remote-mac-control-for-ai-agents/) and [requests for edit buttons](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id364876095). The source check found no use of Accessibility element APIs today, so this is new work. *Why rivals lag:* protocol-bound VNC clients cannot do this against an arbitrary server, and it requires a companion plus a custom message channel. *Effort:* M. *Main risk:* Accessibility coverage is uneven in Electron and creative apps, so it needs a graceful fallback, and users on a large iPad may not care.

**4. Rescue Mode with a Trip Check.** One screen shows route, FileVault status, Remote Login, power-on setting, sleep and permissions, and runs a real outside-network test before you leave home. A rebooted Mac then gets a guided SSH unlock that hands off to Standard VNC and on to Glassy Stream. *Insight:* a traveller [asked for exactly this reachability check](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912), and users assemble [Tailscale, SSH, display tools and restart utilities](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/tahoe-and-filevault/42942) to get the same outcome. *Why rivals lag:* it is unglamorous, cross-subsystem work that must be retested on each macOS release, and it fits a Mac-only product better than cross-platform rivals. *Effort:* M for the check, M to L for unlock. *Main risk:* the client handles a very sensitive admin credential, so never store it by default; [Ethernet reports](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/09/22/tahoe-filevault-icloud-keychain-and-ssh/) and [Wi-Fi fixes](https://www.jeffgeerling.com/blog/2025/you-can-finally-manage-macs-filevault-remotely-tahoe/) differ across versions, and an embedded SSH client needs an App Review check.

**5. Rewind: see what happened while you were away.** With explicit opt-in, the host keeps a short, encrypted, on-device low-frame-rate buffer of a chosen display, and connecting offers a scrubbable catch-up. *Insight:* the worst moment for supervision is a [wedged agent whose chat surface has gone offline](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/remote-connections); [agent-made recordings](https://dev.to/juandastic/my-coding-agents-now-have-a-remote-mac-16fo) exist only if the agent survives. *Why rivals lag:* the feature suits an owned H.264 pipeline, and products built around a vendor relay will find continuous recording harder to explain. *Effort:* M to L. *Main risk:* this records secrets and invites justified privacy concern. It needs retention limits and pausing during secure input, is off by default, and could damage the trust advantage if handled badly.

**6. Select anything on the stream.** Live Text on a frozen frame lets a user copy an error message from the stream with no clipboard channel, including on view-only devices and over Standard VNC. *Insight:* video locks text in place, and Mac-to-phone copying is a known gap. *Why rivals lag:* honestly, they do not, since this is easy to copy; it earns its place because it is cheap and useful. *Effort:* S. *Main risk:* accuracy on compressed small text, so it complements the real clipboard and does not replace it.

**7. Pocket Mode: your Mac as an audio session.** After system audio ships, a user can drop to audio only with the screen off, Now Playing controls and very low data use. Agents that speak and music servers become listenable. *Insight:* [headless music libraries](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1663047912) are an observed use, [Glass surfaces media controls](https://glassdesk.dev/), and [sessions dying on app switch](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758788573) is a shared complaint. *Why rivals lag:* Screens cannot, per its [RFB limit](https://edovia.com/en/screens/); Jump could, but its [battery reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id364876095) make background modes sensitive. *Effort:* S to M after audio. *Main risk:* Apple's background audio rules require real audio, and demand is speculative.

**8. Scam-proof Family Circle, with posture-aware Duo as a sibling bet.** Family Circle allows help only between devices paired while together, with no codes to read over a phone and no incoming-stranger flow. *Insight:* [family support recurs](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id692035811), and [58 of AnyDesk's 150 recent reviews are scam warnings](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1176131273); no direct request for this design was found. *Why rivals lag:* stranger-support flows are the core business for AnyDesk and TeamViewer, though [Screens Assist](https://edovia.com/en/screens/) already exists. *Effort:* M to L, and it depends on outside reach. *Main risk:* support burden and a small paying audience. Separately, Duo could use the [cover screen as a pocket peek](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50302121) and the inner screen as a workspace on a stable virtual display. Effort is M, with the risks being a small installed base and Mac windows rearranging on fold if the display resizes.

## Thin evidence limits how far these rankings should be trusted

**No Reddit thread was read**, because reddit.com blocked every tool, and X was also unreachable. r/macmini, r/ipad, r/homelab and similar communities are therefore unrepresented, and "absent here" means "not checked there". The review sample covers 801 reviews from the US store since 2024, of which 485 rated 3 stars or lower, using capped feeds, uneven time windows and approximate keyword matching. Those counts show themes, not rates or market shares. RealVNC blocked direct fetches, no current Chrome Remote Desktop listing could be retrieved, and AnyDesk, TeamViewer and some Parsec pages were unreadable, so those products are not fairly assessed here. Jump's support forum items rest on snippets.

**No independent, measured latency comparison exists in these notes** for any tool on the Mac-to-iOS path. Every performance figure, including Glass's 20 ms, Splashtop's 4K60 claim and Jump's frame rates, is a vendor claim, and no 2026 independent head-to-head was found. Competitor feature cells are often absence of evidence. Agent-related demand is also soft: OpenClaw's [247,000 stars and Mac mini shortages](https://thenextweb.com/news/claude-mac-mini-openclaw-ai-agents-shortage) are confounded by a memory squeeze, the only size estimate is a secondhand "several thousand" operators, and no survey shows how often supervisors need the full screen. On the product side, the login-window fallback remains unverified, and the Pencil, Accessibility and transport remarks come from targeted source searches, not a full review.

## Conclusion

The research changes the frame from "which features are missing" to "what happens at the moment a person is needed." Stream quality is converging, and agent vendors have made chat-level supervision free, so the durable value sits in the gap between them: the prompt no chat can answer, the job with no hook, the Mac that rebooted while you were away. Glassy Desk's constraints help here. No account, no media relay and a low lifetime price are hard for relay-funded rivals to match, provided the listing stays exact about what any small alert or rendezvous service can see. The most useful single test is cheap: if remote input cannot click a macOS privacy prompt on Glassy Stream, the "escape hatch" story needs Standard VNC behind it or a narrower claim.

The suggested sequence, with timings as rough judgment for a small team, is below.

| When | Do this | Exit test |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 0-2: prove and tidy | Test synthetic clicks on privacy prompts; test the rebooted-Mac and login-window path; run a timed WAN head-to-head against Jump, Screens and Workbench on one Mac; add `requestReview`; disclose the free limit before host install; publish a security page; run a Glass name check; ship the 80-second tier with its measurement in place | Known answers for prompts, reboot and WAN feel; marketing claims corrected to match |
| Weeks 2-8: keep the promise | Sealed gateway, alerts, Live Activity, `glassy notify`, Claude Code and Codex hook setup; Mac-to-phone clipboard; Live Text; connection doctor; guided Tailscale | An alert opens the right Mac; failures state a cause |
| Weeks 6-16: reach parity | Device-matched virtual display with per-device memory; system audio; separate input and video channels | Sharp headless session on iPhone, iPad and Duo; sound; input stays responsive under video congestion |
| Months 4-6: own the moment | Takeover handback; Screen Sentinel; Rescue Mode with Trip Check; Native Mac menus and a tested key guarantee | Measure alert-to-action rate and free-to-paid conversion from alerts |
| Months 6-9: decide on reach | Direct NAT traversal with a media-free rendezvous; then apply the failure threshold to the opt-in, separately priced relay decision | Outside-connection success rate; listing wording updated before release |
| After that: place small bets | Hybrid encoding, Pencil pressure and window streaming as test results justify; then Pocket Mode, Rewind, Family Circle and Duo posture only with user evidence, including a Reddit pass that this research could not perform | Each bet has a named demand signal first |
